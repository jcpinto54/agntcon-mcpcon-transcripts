## Slide 2 — The internet's answer to interoperability: the autonomous system (AS)
**Text.** A PAGE FROM NETWORKING HISTORY / The internet's answer to interoperability: the autonomous system (AS) / When the internet evolved to a global scale, Networks needed a unit smaller than the whole network and bigger than one router unit of work to scale : an autonomous, boundary-declaring domain. / 01 Each domain kept its own internal architecture — the Autonomous System — AS — No two networks had to be built the same way. / 02 Domains declared only what crossed their boundary — route advertisement / peering policy — Never their internal policy. / 03 Every other domain could rely on that declaration — AS path / accepted routes — Trust without inspection. / © 2026 Nokia

## Slide 3 — Networking standardized what policy could act on not the policy itself
**Text.** THE PATTERN · SHARED SEMANTICS, LOCAL POLICY / Networking standardized what policy could act on not the policy itself. / SENDER DOMAIN — Local export policy, Local execution logic — e.g. BGP local-preference [stays internal, never shared] / THE BOUNDARY — Shared semantics + provenance — e.g. the route advertisement itself [a declared fact] / RECEIVER DOMAIN — Local import policy, Local execution logic — Independently decides admission / BGP never asked every network to agree on why they prefer a route — only on what a route advertisement means. / Standardize the semantics of the crossing, not the policy of the domain. / © 2026 Nokia
**Shows.** A three-box horizontal flow (Sender Domain → The Boundary → Receiver Domain, with the middle box highlighted blue) with arrows between them, illustrating that only the boundary's shared meaning is standardized while each side's internal policy logic stays private.

## Slide 4 — Works within one system. Untested across many.
**Text.** THE PRESENT · AGENTIC AI / Works within one system. ## Slide 5 — Federating Agents Is Not Enough. Federate Domains.
**Text.** PROPOSED PRIMITIVES · INVARIANTS 1 AND 2 / Federating Agents Is Not Enough. Federate Domains. / An Agent Control Domain (ACD), as a first-class unit is the largest entity that can make and enforce coherent assertions about authority, behavior/delegation and data. / BGP · AUTONOMOUS SYSTEM — Not a router. An administrative and control boundary containing routers. Declares what it will and will not transit. The unit of inter-domain reasoning. / AGENT CONTROL DOMAIN (ACD) — Not an agent. An administrative and control boundary within which agents operate. Declares permitted crossing classes. The unit of cross-domain agent reasoning. / INVARIANT 1 — The ACD is the unit of inter-domain control not the agent. / INVARIANT 2 — The boundary requires modeling of crossing classes; the crossing classes will act as permission units as well as control enforcement capabilities that could be advertised. / BEYOND A TOKEN · keyed by domain, the crossing classes are few enough and slow enough to publish and converge. Keyed by agent or task, nothing ever converges. / © 2026 Nokia
**Shows.** A side-by-side comparison box mapping BGP's Autonomous System concept onto the proposed Agent Control Domain (ACD), followed by two black highlight bars stating Invariant 1 and Invariant 2 of the proposed model.

## Slide 6 — One request, three declarations
**Text.** THE CONTROL PLANE, MADE CONCRETE / One request, three declarations / If a domain's interior is opaque, something still has to be declared at its boundary — on behalf of authority, delegation state, and data. / CAE — Crossing Authority Envelope — What is this domain's supported control enforcement scope? Is this domain's control enforcement allowing for a safe crossing? — Declares the delegated authority presented at a single crossing. / DSE — Delegation State Envelope — What may still happen next? — Carries the upstream limits still in force what may happen next, not what already happened. / DCE — Data Constraint Envelope — May this data participate here? — Binds data-use constraints to the data itself, asserted at its source. / © 2026 Nokia
**Shows.** Three colour-coded boxes in sequence (teal CAE with a shield icon, black DSE with a gear icon, blue DCE with a database icon) connected by arrows, each defining one of the three envelope types that together form the proposed control-plane declaration at a domain crossing.

## Slide 7 — Not three fields. Three different lifetimes.
**Text.** WHY ONE TOKEN CAN'T DO THIS / Not three fields. Three different lifetimes. / CAE — Evaluated per crossing, then discarded --> stateless. REVOCATION · expiry mechanism / DSE — Upstream limits carried forward; what remains shapes the next plan --> stateful. REVOCATION · a withdrawal takes effect at the next crossing. / DCE — Issued at the data's source, travels with it, can outlive the chain --> data-bound. REVOCATION · expiry is primary. A withdrawal is an obligation, detectable by reconciliation, not preventable. / A DELEGATION CHAIN, OVER TIME / CHAIN ENDS / CAE allow, DSE allow, DCE deny is coherent — B may call C, the delegation is valid, A's data still may not travel. One token cannot say that, because the three don't live on the same clock. / © 2026 Nokia
**Shows.** A timeline chart with three horizontal rows (CAE, DSE, DCE) plotted against a "chain ends" vertical marker: CAE shown as discrete blue point-events along the timeline (stateless, per-crossing), DSE as a grey bar spanning most of the chain's duration (stateful, carried forward), and DCE as a blue bar extending past the chain-ends marker (data-bound, can outlive the chain) — visually demonstrating the three envelopes' differing lifetimes.

## Slide 8 — A domain cannot self-grant scope it wasn't delegated
**Text.** INVARIANT 3 / A domain cannot self-grant scope it wasn't delegated / ROOT AUTHORITY {A, B, C} / CHILD DOMAIN {A, B} / LEAF DOMAIN {A} / {A, B, C} — SELF-GRANTED — BLOCKED (shown struck through) / PRE-AUTHORIZED SCOPE ACTIVATION / A downstream domain never expands its own authority. / An upstream authority may pre-grant a specific, time-bounded, named scope that activates only under defined conditions — always traceable to an authority that actually held it. ## Slide 9 — Enforcement lives at the boundary, outside the agent's logic

## Slide 11 — Control Envelopes and Networking IP/MPLS headers are similar in operation
**Text.** ENCAPSULATION AND THE INTER-DOMAIN PROTOCOL / Control Envelopes and Networking IP/MPLS headers are similar in operation / A DOMAIN HEADER WRAPS ONE OR MORE AGENT MESSAGES · THE PEER EDGE READS ONLY THE HEADER / THE INTERNET · IP, MPLS, GRE — IP / MPLS HEADER · READ AT EVERY BOUNDARY — Imposed at ingress, popped at egress. Addressed to a network, never to a process inside it. — PAYLOAD · OPAQUE IN TRANSIT — TCP segments and application data, forwarded without being read, understood or rewritten by anyone in between. / THE AGENT STACK · ACDs AND CROSSINGS — AGENT DOMAIN HEADER · READ AT BOTH EDGES — CAE — class, subject, purpose, limits · the DSE chain so far · an DCE digest per data object. — PAYLOAD · OPAQUE TO THE CONTROL PLANE — One or more A2A or MCP messages, carried unmodified [never inspected, never re-signed, never verified by Domain boundary] / HOW THE ANALOGY MAPS · THREE PROPERTIES CARRIED OVER — ADDRESSED TO A DOMAIN, NOT A HOST: The domain header characterizes what the receiving ACD is expected to enforce — IMPOSED ONCE, HONOURED FOR A WHILE: A crossing context authorizes a class once, and bounds the exchange with limits. — OPACITY BY CONSTRUCTION: The core forwards what it cannot read; the peer executes what we do not inspect. / THE PAYOFF · NO PROTOCOL COMPETETION OR DIRECT DEPENDECY / Two layers, one boundary — and no negotiation about what happens on the other side. / © 2026 Nokia
**Shows.** Two parallel stacked boxes comparing the internet's IP/MPLS header model (header read at every boundary, payload opaque in transit) against the proposed agent-domain header model (CAE/DSE/DCE read at both edges, A2A/MCP payload opaque to the control plane), followed by a three-column row explaining which properties of the networking analogy carry over.

## Slide 18 — Part of it is. The part that matters breaks the assumptions in A2A.
**Text.** IS THIS JUST ANOTHER A2A EXTENSION / Part of it is. The part that matters breaks the assumptions in A2A. / WHAT IF IT RIDES ON A2A TODAY: DOMAIN IN THE AGENT CARD · CAE ON MESSAGE SEND · DSE BOUND TO PARTS AND ARTIFACTS / WHERE THE ASSUMPTIONS BREAK / 01 · PAIRWISE BY CONSTRUCTION — A client and a remote agent. A cumulative chain artifact has no owner in a two-party model, and no one is obliged to carry it forward. / 02 · NOT EVERY HOP SPEAKS A2A — Chains run through tool calls, queues, batch jobs and plain APIs. Admission has to be the receiving domain's decision, not a handshake. / THE VERDICT · A GOOD FIRST CARRIER · NOT THE ONLY ONE AND NOT THE BEST ONE· THE ARTIFACTS MUST BE TRANSPORT-INDEPENDENT / Define the semantics once independent of A2A later decide whether we should bind them to A2A. / © 2026 Nokia
