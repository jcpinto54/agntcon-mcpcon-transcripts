THE LINE
A log is enough, until someone else has to believe it.
log()
Good for
debugging
ops dashboards
“what happened last night”
the author’s own memory
A log is the operator’s diary. For the operator,
that’s fine.
Not enough when
money moves
a payment, a trade, a refund
a duty applies
a regulator, a contract, a customer’s right
someone else was harmed
a counterparty, an evaluator, a court
Each of these has a party who was not in the room and does
not take your word.
The moment a second party is entitled to the truth, a
diary is a claim, not evidence.
~1,300 transcripts · 0 timestamps per action (reconstructed) · integrity: “OpenAI attested”
spoofed tool calls: ~7% · “we cannot rule it out” ×3 · METR with Redwood Research, Aug 26 2026
Three investigators, six days, ~$400K, and the answers still end in “cannot rule out.” Not because they were careless. Because every record
was the subject’s own.
What follows is what it takes for a second party to believe a record without trusting its author.

EU AI Act · your role decides what you owe
Provider or deployer?
Provider
Art 3(3)
Develops an AI system or model, or has one developed,
and puts it on the market or into service under its
own name.
Build your own agents? These obligations are yours,
traced to what your agents did.
Deployer
Art 3(4)
Uses an AI system under its own authority, other than
for personal, non-professional use.
Run a vendor’s agent? Hold it to your obligations: ask
for receipts.
Deployer that’s a public body
Extra duties, such as a fundamental-
rights impact assessment before first
use (Art 27).
GPAI model provider
Makes the underlying model. Chapter
V duties since Aug 2025; you receive
its documentation.
Supervised by the AI Office
GPAI-based systems from the model’s
own provider, and systems inside very
large online platforms (Art 75).
Art 25: rebrand a high-risk system, substantially modify one, or repurpose a system into high-risk, and a
deployer becomes its provider.
