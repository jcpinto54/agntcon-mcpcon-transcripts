# For speakers

You are probably here because you gave a talk at **AGNTCon + MCPCon Europe
2026**, and someone told you there is a transcript of it in this repository.
There is. This page explains how it got here and what you can ask for.

**The short version: it's your talk. If you want it changed, it gets changed.
If you want it gone, it gets removed — no argument, no justification asked
for.** Skip to [What you can ask for](#what-you-can-ask-for).

## What this is

Five rooms ran in parallel across two days, so nobody at that conference saw
more than a fifth of it. A handful of attendees recorded the sessions they sat
in, and this archive turns those recordings into text that people can search,
read and quote. It currently holds 19 of the 93 sessions.

It exists because a talk that only exists as a memory is a talk that stops
being useful about a week after it is given.

## What was done to your talk

- **An attendee recorded it from the audience** — on a phone, not from the
  desk. If the sound was poor, the transcript will show it.
- **It was transcribed with Whisper large-v3**, in almost every case on the
  contributor's own machine, so the recording was never uploaded anywhere.
- **Your name, your company and the talk title were corrected** against the
  official conference programme rather than trusted from what the software
  heard. Speech recognition is unkind to names.
- **A short background note on you** sits above the transcript, written from
  your bio in the programme and public sources.
- **Nothing else was edited.** Your grammar was not smoothed, your phrasing was
  not tightened, your filler was not cut. The archive's rule is that a
  transcript is what you said, not somebody's improvement on it.

## What was deliberately not done

- **Your audio is not published, and never will be.** Recordings stay on the
  machines of the people who made them. Every audio format is blocked in
  `.gitignore`, and that is the one rule of this project that has no exceptions.
- **Your slides are not here.** They are yours to publish.
- **Your talk was not summarised on top of your words.** Where a summary
  exists, it sits in a separate file, labelled as derived, so that a reader
  always knows whether they are reading you or someone's reading of you. Where
  the two disagree, your words win and the summary is the thing that gets
  fixed.
- **Your attribution was not guessed.** With five parallel rooms, a timestamp
  alone rarely identifies a talk. Where the archive was not certain which
  session a recording came from, it says so in the file rather than picking the
  likeliest answer.

## What you can ask for

Three things, and you never have to explain why.

### 1. Correct something

Mistranscribed names, mangled protocol terms, a garbled product name, a wrong
company, a background note that gets your role wrong. These are the most
common problems in the archive and the most welcome to fix.

### 2. Add something

A link to your slides, to the official recording, to the post you wrote
afterwards, or to your own corrected transcript if you have one. Anything that
sends a reader from your talk to you.

If you have a better recording of your own talk than the one from the fourth
row, that is very welcome too — and it would replace the current transcript
rather than sit beside it.

### 3. Have it removed

If you would rather your talk were not here, say so and it comes down. You do
not need a reason, you do not need to be polite about it, and you will not be
talked out of it or asked to reconsider. The commit history is rewritten too,
so it is not merely hidden behind a link.

This offer is not conditional on anything and does not expire.

## How to ask

- **[Open an issue](../../issues/new?template=speaker-request.md)** — there is
  a template for exactly this. Quickest route.
- **Open a pull request**, if the fix is small and you would rather just make
  it.
- **Not a GitHub user?** Any of the contact routes on
  [@jcpinto54](https://github.com/jcpinto54)'s profile will reach me.

Removals are actioned as a priority. Corrections are usually same-week.

## One thing that would help

If your transcript looks right to you, saying so — in an issue, or anywhere
at all — is genuinely useful. A speaker confirming their own transcript is the
strongest signal this archive can carry that it is faithful, and it is worth
more than anything the person who transcribed it can say about it.

And if you happen to have a recording of a *different* session you sat in on:
[the coverage table](README.md#coverage) shows the 74 sessions still missing.
