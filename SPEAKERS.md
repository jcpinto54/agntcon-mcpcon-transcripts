# For speakers

You spoke at **AGNTCon + MCPCon Europe 2026**. This page is addressed to you.

Five rooms ran in parallel across two days, so nobody at that conference saw
more than a fifth of it. This archive is an attempt to fix that after the
fact — attendees transcribed the sessions they happened to sit in, and the
result is text people can search, read and quote. It currently holds
transcripts for 19 of the 93 sessions.

So one of two things is true for your talk, and this page covers both.

> **The short version.** It's your talk. If you have anything at all — slides,
> a link, a recording, references you put up — we would love to have it, and
> you will be credited. If something here is wrong, it gets fixed. If you want
> your talk out of the archive entirely, it comes out, no reason needed and no
> argument made.

## The part we'd actually like to ask you about

**Whatever you have, we want it.** This is the real reason to get in touch,
and it applies to all 93 speakers whether or not anyone recorded you.

A transcript is a poor substitute for what you already have on your own disk.
Anything in this list is worth more than the thing an archive can produce by
itself:

- **Your slides.** A PDF, a link, a deck already public — whatever form.
- **The references you cited.** The papers, repos, specs and posts behind the
  talk. These are often the single most useful thing and the first thing lost.
- **Your own recording**, if you or your team captured one. Better audio than
  the fourth row, and where it exists it replaces the attendee transcript
  rather than sitting beside it.
- **Anything you wrote up afterwards** — the blog post, the thread, the
  follow-up talk, the docs page the talk was really about.
- **A transcript you already have.** Some speakers have one from their own
  captioning. Yours beats ours, every time.
- **Corrections**, if your talk is here and something in it is wrong.

None of this obliges you to anything else. Sending slides is not agreeing to
have a transcript, and a talk with nothing but a link to your deck is a
genuinely useful entry in this archive.

Material you share lands in `talks/<slug>/materials.md`, credited to you and
marked as coming from the speaker rather than scraped from somewhere. It shows
up in the [coverage table](README.md#coverage) alongside the transcripts, and
it works perfectly well for a session that has no recording at all.

## If your talk is not in the archive

This is the likely case — 74 of the 93 sessions have no transcript. It means
nobody who recorded anything was sitting in your room, and nothing more than
that. Attendees could only capture the talks they chose to attend.

The archive holds your session's title, time, room, speakers and abstract from
the conference programme, and that is all. It does not pretend the abstract is
the talk; an abstract is what you said you would say, months earlier.

If you would like your session to be more than a row in a schedule, the list
above is how. Slides alone are enough to make it a real entry.

## If your talk is in the archive

Here is exactly what was done to it:

- **An attendee recorded it from the audience** — on a phone, not from the
  desk. Where the sound was poor, the transcript shows it.
- **It was transcribed with Whisper large-v3**, in almost every case on the
  contributor's own machine, so your recording was never uploaded anywhere.
- **Your name, company and talk title were corrected** against the official
  programme rather than trusted from what the software heard. Speech
  recognition is unkind to names.
- **A short background note on you** sits above the transcript, written from
  your programme bio and public sources.
- **Nothing else was edited.** Your grammar was not smoothed, your phrasing
  not tightened, your filler not cut. The rule here is that a transcript is
  what you said, not somebody's improvement on it.

And what was deliberately *not* done:

- **Your audio is not published, and never will be.** Recordings stay on the
  machines of the people who made them. Every audio format is blocked in
  `.gitignore`, and that is the one rule of this project with no exceptions.
- **Your slides are not here** unless you put them here. They are yours.
- **Your talk was not summarised on top of your words.** Where a summary
  exists it sits in a separate file, labelled as derived, so a reader always
  knows whether they are reading you or someone's reading of you. Where the
  two disagree, your words win and the summary is what gets fixed.
- **Your attribution was not guessed.** With five parallel rooms a timestamp
  rarely identifies a talk on its own. Where the archive was not certain which
  session a recording came from, the file says so rather than picking the
  likeliest answer.

## If you want it taken down

Say so and it comes down.

You do not need a reason, you do not need to be polite about it, and you will
not be talked out of it or asked to reconsider. The commit history is rewritten
too, so it is not merely hidden behind a dead link. This offer is not
conditional on anything and does not expire.

It also applies to any part of it — the transcript but not the slides, the
summary but not the transcript, your bio note, whatever you like.

## How to reach us

- **[Open an issue](../../issues/new?template=speaker-request.md)** — there is
  a template for exactly this, covering both sharing material and asking for
  something to change or go. Quickest route.
- **Open a pull request**, if you would rather just make the change yourself.
- **Not a GitHub user?** Any of the contact routes on
  [@jcpinto54](https://github.com/jcpinto54)'s profile will reach me, and
  sending a link by any means at all is fine. Do not let the tooling be the
  obstacle.

Removals are actioned as a priority. Everything else is usually same-week.

## One small thing that helps

If your transcript looks right to you, saying so — in an issue, or anywhere at
all — is genuinely useful. A speaker confirming their own transcript is the
strongest signal this archive can carry that it is faithful, and it is worth
more than anything the person who transcribed it could say about it.

And if you happened to record a *different* session you sat in on, the
[coverage table](README.md#coverage) shows the 74 still missing. Speakers
attend other people's talks too.
